## Grok

$$\pi^2 = E \cdot m - c$$

is the relation as written.

In SI units this is dimensionally inconsistent: the left side is dimensionless, $E \cdot m$ has units of energy × mass (J·kg), and $c$ has units of speed (m/s). The three quantities cannot be added or subtracted.

Possible intended rearrangements that restore consistency (none follows from the words alone):

- $E = \pi^2 m c^2$ ($\pi^2$ simply scales the Einstein rest energy)
- $E = \dfrac{m c^2}{\pi^2}$
- $\pi^2 = \dfrac{E}{m c^2}$ (a dimensionless ratio)

Grok asked which form was intended and what physical system it describes.
